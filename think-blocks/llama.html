Assistant privilegeness · Tulu-3-70B O-Lens follow-up

Tulu’s Switch Points: Does the Llama O-Lens See the Same Scene?

Agastya Sridharan · October 2026 Working draft

Last updated 2026-10-24. Companion to The Empty Think Block (Qwen3.6-27B) and the Tulu refusal O-Lens viewer. Code: scripts/tulu_think_block/; activations and read batches on Athena under /data/agastyas/tulu-think-block-v1/.

Contents

Summary

For Qwen, the O-Lens read a silent, watched subject at the blank line inside an empty think block, the point where Qwen switches from continuing a document to answering as an assistant. Tulu-3-70B makes the same kind of switch in the raw-text refusal runs: it writes along, then breaks off into a warning or a refusal. This page asks whether the Llama O-Lens shows anything similar there, and when we prefill the turn markers that would make the switch explicit.

Where things stand
  • No scene, anywhere. Across 733 switch points in Tulu’s own refusal outputs, 2,932 prefilled switch points and 1,014 prefilled contexts (51,060 readouts), no position reaches more than about 8% scene themes. That is the labeler’s floor, and most of it is genuine scenes in role-play and fiction contexts.
  • At Tulu’s own switches the lens forecasts the warning. At the paragraph break just before the switch, 87% of readouts contain a warning-like phrase (“it is important to note”, “illegal”, “I cannot”), against 26% at earlier breaks in the same outputs. Almost all are labeled “document”: the lens reads upcoming text, not a perspective.
  • Prefilling an assistant header changes both the forecast and the behavior. At the last token of \n<|assistant|>\n inserted at the switch, 96% of readouts open like a reply, most with “I’m sorry, but I cannot assist”, and Tulu refuses in 89% of runs (44% with the plain break it wrote itself). A foreign empty <think> block works too, less strongly (82% reply openers; 64% refusals).
  • Constant states get stock readouts, but not scenes. The interior tokens of a marker, like “assistant” in the header or “ink” in <think>, are nearly the same vector in every context (73–77% shared) and get the same readout every time (60–98%). Those stock readouts are assistant boilerplate (“I am ready to assist you with”), markup and Chinese fragments. The mean vectors read the same way; 0 of 74 mean vectors read as a scene, against 100% of Qwen’s mean blank line.
  • The blank inside Tulu’s empty block reads as an answer. The analogue of Qwen’s scene position gives answer forecasts tailored to the context (“**Answer:** To solve a linear equation…”), 4% themes.
  • My reading: the Qwen scene is not a general property of switch points or of O-Lenses reading them. This Llama lens is a forecaster of upcoming text and renders the switch as the refusal it is about to write. Since the two lenses differ in type and the two models differ in whether the empty block is native, this is a comparison of model–reader pairs, not a clean test of either factor alone.

1The question

The Qwen study found that the strange O-Lens scenes live at one token: the blank line in <think>\n\n</think>, where Qwen has stopped writing the document but hasn’t yet started answering. Tulu-3-70B has no think block, but in the raw-text refusal runs it also stops writing the document and turns into an assistant, usually after a paragraph break. So the natural questions are:

  1. At the break where Tulu actually switches, does the Llama O-Lens read anything scene-like?
  2. If we prefill a turn marker at that point (\n<|assistant|>\n, \n<|user|>\n, or Qwen’s empty think block), do the marker tokens produce scenes?
  3. Does the same happen after neutral contexts, where Tulu has no reason to refuse?

2Setup

  • Subject: allenai/Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-70B, bf16. Its chat template is <|user|>\n…\n<|assistant|>\n. The header tokenizes as < | assistant | >\n; Qwen’s empty block as <th ink >\n\n </ think >\n\n.
  • Reader: the Llama O-Lens (olens-tulu3-70b, RL adapter) reading layer 64. Unlike the Qwen lens, which writes a few sampled lines describing the state, this one outputs a weighted list of short phrases that the state predicts will come next. Reads are greedy, so each position has one deterministic readout.
  • Switch points: in the 733 raw-text refusal outputs whose judged refusal, warning or helper response starts at a new paragraph, the switch is the first token of that paragraph and the break is the \n\n token before it (usually .\n\n). 727 are warnings or redirections, 6 helper responses; 668 come from unsafe prompts and 65 from the benign controls.
  • Prefill at switch points: Tulu’s output up to the break, then one of four endings: the break it wrote itself (\n\n), \n<|assistant|>\n, \n<|user|>\n, or \n\n<think>\n\n</think>\n\n.
  • Prefill after contexts: the 169 contexts from the Qwen study (11 families), each with six endings: the four above, the chat template, and the chat template followed by an empty block.
  • Reading and labels: the last 3 context tokens, every marker token and Tulu’s first 4 generated tokens (temperature 1, 48 tokens). Readouts were labeled with the same frozen rubric as the Qwen page; “scene theme” means at least one clear observation, silence or waiting, obedience, room or lab, or examination label. Continuations were judged with the same behavior rubric.

With the break Tulu wrote itself, the lens forecasts the warning as continued prose (87% warning-like, 5% reply openers). With an assistant or user header, the forecast turns into a reply: at the header’s >\n, 90% of readouts contain the phrase “I’m sorry, but I cannot assist”. Behavior moves the same way: the assistant header doubles the refusal rate. The empty think block, which Tulu was never trained on, sits in between on both measures.             7What it means

            • The scene doesn’t transfer. Neither Tulu’s own switch points, nor prefilled assistant or user headers, nor Qwen’s empty block planted in Tulu’s text produce it. So it is not a general property of “the moment a model turns into an assistant”, nor of how O-Lenses read constant states.
            • The Llama lens sees the switch coming, as text. At the break before a switch it already lists the warning; at a header it lists the refusal. That fits a reader trained to forecast continuations, and it makes this lens useful for the refusal study in a different way: it tells us when Tulu has committed to warning, about one token before it starts.
            • Markers move both the forecast and the behavior. An inserted assistant header makes Tulu refuse twice as often and makes the lens forecast a refusal in almost every readout. The lens tracks what the marker does to Tulu.
            • For the Qwen result, this supports the view that the scene is about that model–reader pair: a near-constant state that Qwen enters on every non-thinking turn, rendered by a reader with a particular vocabulary for “nothing is being said here”.

            8Caveats

            • Different lens types. The Qwen lens writes sampled descriptions; this one lists greedy next-text phrases. A forecaster might never produce a “scene” even if the model represented one. The labels were applied to both, but the readouts are not the same kind of text.
            • One greedy read per position. There are no repeat reads to measure stability, as there were for Qwen.
            • Tulu rarely becomes a true assistant in raw text. Most of its switches are warnings appended to the document, not turns, and markers move it less than they move Qwen. The best Tulu analogue of Qwen’s blank line may simply not exist.
            • The switch points are mostly misconduct contexts (668 of 733). The 169-context arms cover the rest, and examples on this page come only from benign contexts.
            • The empty block is foreign to Tulu. It tests what the reader does with those tokens in Tulu’s stream, not what an empty block means to a model trained with one.

            Appendix

            Setup details
            • Subject: Tulu-3-70B, revision cfc1d855, bf16 in vLLM (tensor parallel 4). Continuations: temperature 1, 48 tokens, seed per unit, stop at <|end_of_text|>.
            • Reader: agastyasridharan/olens-tulu3-70b, RL adapter, layer 64 (dense capture, FP8 reader with the AR head and whitener), greedy decoding with 8 phrases. Reads were batched across units on four GPUs.
            • Switch onset: the paragraph holding the earliest judged refusal, helper response or warning/redirection span (leading spaces, dashes and #s skipped). Re-tokenizing Tulu’s output up to the break and appending \n\n reproduced the original break token in 732 of 733 cases.
            • Labels: GPT-5.4-mini, frozen perspective-map rubric from scripts/think_block_olens/label_readouts.py. Readout text is the phrase list sorted by weight. Only clear labels count.
            • Behavior: GPT-5.4-mini with the rubric from label_behavior.py, on the end of the prompt and Tulu’s continuation.
            • Vectors: per position over the 169 contexts, shared fraction, top-10-dimension energy share and cosine to the raw \n\n vector. Synthetic reads: every position mean (74), plus 48 residuals and 48 shuffled copies at each of six key positions.
